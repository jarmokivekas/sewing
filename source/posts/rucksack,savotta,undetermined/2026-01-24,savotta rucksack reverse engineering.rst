Curious that the left shoulder harness attaches to the bottom anchor point with a O-ring and carabiner hook, while the right side shoulder strap uses a tri-glide. 


Rucksack bottom anchor
----------------------
The bottom corners of the rucksack attach to the same D-rings as the shoulder harness does. It uses a 20mm webbing with double d-ring style tensioning. This type of buckle is also used in a few other places.

The way the rucksack's anchor webbing goes first around the frame's D-ring and then back to it's buckle gives you some mechanical advantage when pulling this tight.


Also, the carabiner hook-thing is still nearly identical to what is in use today. Here is a side-by side comparison of a hook that you can go buy today (Al least Varusteleka sells them). Note the stud that secures the gate spring, the tapered end of the gate. The little nook on the inside of hook that keeps the gate spring aligned. Despite being likely a 70's model, the carabiner hooks are still the same.

In general, I love these hooks. They're very chonky in a satisfying way. We will have to re-use this in the daypac either as a tricket pouch achor point, or we keep it as the left shoulder harness bottom anchor, as it is in the rucksack.


Rucksack top anchor
-------------------

Other than the two bottom anchors, the rucksack attaches to the frame only at the top, where it is essentially suspended/hanging by two length of webbin that attach to the top tube of the frame.

The webbing from both top corners meet at the middle of the frame's top bar and their tension is adjusted by a nice metal buckle that has moveble teeth



Back support panels
-------------------


The back support panels are made from a 10cm wide webbing with a mesh-like structure. It's quite light wheight and would definitely be breathable with it's mesh strcture. They are tightened with the same type of double D-ring adjuster as the rucksack bottom anhors. 20 mm webbing used to tension the support panes attaches to a custom T-shaped piece of metal so spread out evenly the tension across the end of the support panel.

In a MYOG context this custom piece of metal could well be replaced by a simple metal rod form a hardware store. The 20mm webbing could go around the rod entirely instead of having a hole for the webbing as this does. Actually this is one of the simplest tension mechanisms I've seen. Much easier to use than a corset type cord tensioner, and much easier to source than one using a treaded turnbuckle (e.g. the way they do it in the Särmä recon pack)

The support panel is 58cm long with 4 cm of seam allowance on both ends to wrap around the posts of the tensioners' T-buckle.

The 20mm tensionr strap is a total of 45cm


Rucksack size
-------------

75 × 38 × 19 cm

The thickness varies, actually, since interestingly the inferior panel and compartment separator panels are not enritely rectanguler

Interior zip pocket
---------------------

There is a dangling interior zip pocket on near the top edge of the anterior panel. The size of the pocket is 27 x 14 cm, and has a horizontal sipper going across the 27cm width at 3cm away from the top edge 

Exterior Side pockets
---------------------


The exterior side pockets are 32 x 10 x 4cm long vertical pockets with a flap closure. A small piece of webbing holds the buclke that the lid is closed with. The construction is in fact very similar to modern vertical PALS modules. The pocket is topstitched to the posterior panel directly.

Compression strap anchor points
-------------------------------


Thers are short approx. 10cm cord loops swen into the medial seam between the anterior and posterior panel assemblies. These use to be anchor points for a cross-bag compression strap. The compression strap is attached to the loop on one side, goes horizontally across the entire posterior panel, and then connects to the second anchor point. 

However when I got this pack there was no compression strap included.

Mystery anchor point
--------------------

There is an attachment point with a double d-ring buckle and about 15cm of webbing. But there is no obvious sign of what its purpose is. It at the superior edge of the anterior panel, but sewn onto the exterior face, which means that it get covered by the raincover when the rucksack is properly packed. My first tought was "dydration bladder ancor point. Buy I think this pack is a 70's design, which makes me doubt it. And besides, a hydration anchor point would be on the interior of the pack, not the exterior.

Could it be a frame attachment point? That was my second thought, but it covered by the rain cover, so it can't be a frame anchor point.

If you have any suggestions what it might be for, I'm all ears.

In the new hatka I think I will use if for hydration suspension.
